# What China means by "artificial intelligence" — cosmotechnics, shanzhai and the state

**Source:** [Altri Orienti Ep.176 — "Cosa intende la Cina per intelligenza artificiale"](https://podcasttranscript.ai/library/ep-176-cosa-intende-la-cina-per-intelligenza)
**Author:** Simone Pirani (Altri Orienti, Chora Media podcast)

---

## Summary

The episode argues that "artificial intelligence" does not mean the same thing in China as in the West, and that you cannot understand AI from a single geographic vantage point. Building on the 2025 book *Machine Decision is not final* and Yuk Hui's *Cosmotechnics*, it traces the difference through DeepSeek, the Chinese language, Buddhist and Legalist traditions, and the Chinese state's own AI narrative, while refusing the essentialist shortcut that "the Chinese are just like that" (source: 2026-06-27-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Cosa intende la Cina per "intelligenza artificiale".md).

## DeepSeek's three moves

The editors of *Machine Decision is not final* read the January 2025 DeepSeek shock as an indicator of something larger than an economic event, articulated in three moves (source: 2026-06-27-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Cosa intende la Cina per "intelligenza artificiale".md):

1. **Beating the chip story.** The accepted narrative was that frontier AI needs the most powerful chips in enormous quantities, a race won by compute and money (OpenAI as the example). With the US blocking exports of advanced chips to China, the conclusion seemed obvious: China stays behind. DeepSeek built a model competitive with Silicon Valley's best on older, weaker hardware by working on software rather than brute force, consuming and costing less, and bypassing the obstacle meant to keep it out. It demonstrated that algorithmic efficiency can reshape the relationship between capital and model performance (source: 2026-06-27-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Cosa intende la Cina per "intelligenza artificiale".md).
2. **Shanzhai.** China had done this before. *Shanzhai* (literally "mountain fortress", a term from the classic *Water Margin* connoting revolt against central authority) became the practice of fast, cheap, transformed copying — not mere imitation but a reversal where the final product becomes something new, fit for the local market. Seen first with Shenzhen mobile phones twenty years ago, now with AI (source: 2026-06-27-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Cosa intende la Cina per "intelligenza artificiale".md).
3. **Open-source release.** DeepSeek was released open-source; once out, the technology cannot be recalled or controlled — anyone (e.g. Perplexity) can take and use it (source: 2026-06-27-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Cosa intende la Cina per "intelligenza artificiale".md).

The editors warn against framing this as a two-nation race (US vs China, flag against flag); doing so misses the point. The likelier trajectory is a multiplication of models and even two different conceptions of what technology is (source: 2026-06-27-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Cosa intende la Cina per "intelligenza artificiale".md).

## Cosmotechnics (Yuk Hui)

Hong Kong philosopher Yuk Hui's *Cosmotechnics: the question of technology in China* (Italian edition 2021, Nero) starts from Heidegger, who held that modern technique is a way of seeing the world that reduces everything to a standing reserve of resources (a river becomes hydroelectric energy, a forest becomes timber). Heidegger sought an exit by returning to the Greeks; Hui asks why one always returns to the Greeks, when other worlds and other histories exist (source: 2026-06-27-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Cosa intende la Cina per "intelligenza artificiale".md).

**Cosmotechnics** is defined as the unification of cosmic order and moral order through technical activity: for most of human history, making tools was never neutral, but bound to an idea of how the world is and what is good. Hui's subtler point is that the West *also* has a cosmotechnics — a worldview separating nature from man — and its error is not having the wrong technique but having *forgotten that its technique is one choice among others* and mistaking it for universal. China, he argues, had a real cosmotechnics binding *qi* (tools) and *dao* (the way), but the link broke when China imported Western science and technique in a hurry across the 19th–20th centuries to avoid being crushed — keeping the tools without the cosmology. The stake is **technodiversity**: as there is biodiversity, there should be a plurality of ways of conceiving technology (source: 2026-06-27-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Cosa intende la Cina per "intelligenza artificiale".md).

## The language and the Musk–Jack Ma gap

In Chinese, AI is *rengong zhineng* — *ren* (person) + *gong* (work) = "made by man", plus *zhineng* (intelligence). Where the Western "artificial" connotes the non-natural, fabricated, even fake or alien, the Chinese term reads closer to "human-made intelligence" — an extension of human work, something created by us and lived alongside, not an alien arrival. The 2019 World AI Conference panel where Elon Musk (AI as existential threat to the species) and Jack Ma (AI as opportunity, a next step humans made and will manage) talked past each other dramatises the gap: Musk's fear sits inside the word "artificial", Ma's optimism inside *rengong* (source: 2026-06-27-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Cosa intende la Cina per "intelligenza artificiale".md).

## Buddhism, Legalism, and the anti-essentialist turn

In a certain Mahayana Buddhist tradition, Buddha-nature belongs not only to humans but in some form to non-sentient things, so a mind inside a machine is not the violation of a sacred boundary it is in the West (the episode cites a 19th-century German critic calling photography blasphemous for capturing the image of God). Buddhism never feared communication technologies — it adopted printing centuries before Gutenberg to reproduce sacred texts — and the editors use the phrase "technologies of salvation" running from print to photography to Dharma chatbots and praying robots (source: 2026-06-27-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Cosa intende la Cina per "intelligenza artificiale".md).

The episode then deliberately dismantles the essentialist trap ("the Chinese are spiritual, so they accept AI"). Essentialism explains 1.4 billion people by an immutable historical essence, and it errs in both directions — the romantic "spiritual East" version and the "tech-enabled yellow peril" version. The differences are real but they are the product of history, a state, and specific choices, not an essence. The key historical driver may be less Buddhism than China's long tradition of **Legalism and bureaucracy** — governing as an administrative machine that registers, classifies, controls and surveils, for which AI is not a rupture but the latest instrument of government-through-data. And the optimism is also manufactured: Beijing made AI a national banner, set the goal of world leadership, funded it, created university courses, and built a propaganda narrative of technological redemption — proof the country is no longer the one that "only copied" (source: 2026-06-27-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Cosa intende la Cina per "intelligenza artificiale".md).

## Conclusion

The episode's answer: China means by AI "a machine that is not an alien but an extension of man, inside a culture that fears it less, in the hands of a state that uses it as a banner of redemption." Not mysticism, not a stately monolith, but a **field of forces** where different actors pull in different directions, each drawing on a different piece of Chinese history (spiritual and bureaucratic). The deeper payoff is reflexive: if China shows AI can be conceived otherwise, then the Western conception is also just one choice made at a point in history — debatable and changeable, not a universal truth about technique (source: 2026-06-27-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Cosa intende la Cina per "intelligenza artificiale".md).

## Key Takeaways

- DeepSeek's significance is read as three moves: algorithmic efficiency beating the chip-export blockade, the *shanzhai* tradition of transformative copying, and uncontrollable open-source release.
- Yuk Hui's *cosmotechnics* frames technology as always bound to a culture's cosmic and moral order; the West's error is forgetting its own view is a choice, not a universal — the stake being technodiversity.
- The Chinese term *rengong zhineng* ("human-made intelligence") carries a less alien, less threatening relation to the machine than the Western "artificial".
- Chinese AI optimism is best explained not by an essential cultural trait but by history, a Legalist/bureaucratic governing tradition, and a deliberate state narrative of national redemption.
