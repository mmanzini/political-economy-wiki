# The Anthropic Institute — research agenda on AI's societal impact

**Source:** [Focus areas for The Anthropic Institute](https://www.anthropic.com/research/anthropic-institute-agenda)
**Author:** Anthropic (The Anthropic Institute, agenda last updated 7 May 2026)

---

## Summary

The Anthropic Institute (TAI) sets out the questions driving its research: using information accessible only from inside a frontier lab to investigate AI's impact on the world and publish findings so external organisations, governments and the public can make better decisions. The rationale mirrors Core Views on AI Safety — effective impact research, like safety research, requires close contact with frontier systems. TAI's work is expected to increasingly feed Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust (source: 2026-07-16-Focus areas for The Anthropic Institute.md).

## Four pillars

- **Economic diffusion.** Who adopts AI and who captures its value (development concentrated in few companies/countries, deployment global); firm-level adoption effects on industrial organisation ("if a 3-person team can now do what required 300"); whether AI follows general-purpose-technology adoption patterns; productivity growth; redistribution mechanisms; labour-market impacts including the professional-pipeline problem (if AI absorbs the junior tasks that built expertise, where does senior judgment come from?) and the role of paid work if AI reduces its centrality. Asks whether AI diffusion could be modulated with central-bank-style "dials" (source: 2026-07-16-Focus areas for The Anthropic Institute.md).
- **Threats and resilience.** Dual-use capability observability (health tools enable surveillance; coding enables hacking); market-driven risk pricing; the offense-defense balance in cyber/bio and conflict; crisis infrastructure analogous to the Cold War hotline (possibly company-to-state or company-to-company); closing the gap between months-scale capability advances and years-scale regulatory response; AI's effect on the cost and efficacy of surveillance. Feeds the Frontier Red Team agenda (source: 2026-07-16-Focus areas for The Anthropic Institute.md).
- **AI systems in the wild.** Group epistemology when a population consults the same few models; detecting critical-thinking degradation; interfaces that promote human agency; managing mixed human-AI teams; behavioural effects à la social media; transparency regimes for external researchers; measuring model "values" against training constitutions; governing autonomous agents (e.g. naval law on abandoned ships as an analogy), agent identity and reliability, AI governing AI, and emergent agent-to-agent norms (source: 2026-07-16-Focus areas for The Anthropic Institute.md).
- **AI-driven R&D.** Governance and visibility over AI systems developing successor systems; "fire drill" tabletop exercises for an intelligence explosion; telemetry to measure aggregate AI-R&D speed as an early-warning signal for recursive self-improvement; intervention points for slowing an explosion and who should wield them; the uneven "tech tree" of AI-accelerated science and the jagged frontier of underinvested high-externality domains (drug discovery, materials) (source: 2026-07-16-Focus areas for The Anthropic Institute.md).

## Commitments and mechanics

- TAI will share more granular, higher-cadence Economic Index data as an early-warning signal for labour disruption; research on societal resilience investment priorities; and detail on how AI tools have sped up Anthropic's own work, with implications for recursive self-improvement (source: 2026-07-16-Focus areas for The Anthropic Institute.md).
- TAI is meant to shape Anthropic's decisions — sharing data it otherwise would not, or altering release approaches (cyber threat analyses feeding Project Glasswing). The agenda is explicitly living, developed with the LTBT, and staffed partly through the four-month Anthropic Fellows programme (source: 2026-07-16-Focus areas for The Anthropic Institute.md).

## Key Takeaways

- TAI institutionalises the claim that impact research, like safety research, is lab-positional: the vantage point is the asset, and publishing it is framed as a public good.
- The professional-pipeline question and "diffusion dials" are the most policy-loaded items — both imply frontier labs actively co-managing labour-market transition speed with governments.
- The AI-driven R&D pillar treats recursive self-improvement as a measurable, drillable operational risk rather than an abstraction — telemetry, fire drills, intervention points.
