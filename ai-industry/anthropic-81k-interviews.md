# What 81,000 people want from AI — Anthropic's interview study

**Source:** [What 81,000 people want from AI](https://www.anthropic.com/features/81k-interviews)
**Author:** Anthropic

---

## Summary

Over one week in December, Anthropic invited every Claude.ai account holder to a conversational interview with Anthropic Interviewer (Claude prompted as interviewer); 80,508 people across 159 countries and 70 languages participated — claimed as the largest and most multilingual qualitative study ever conducted. Claude-powered classifiers coded each conversation for what people want from AI, whether they're getting it, and what they fear (source: 2026-07-16-What 81,000 people want from AI.md).

## What people want

Nine hope clusters: professional excellence (18.8%), personal transformation (13.7%), life management (13.5%), time freedom (11.1%), financial independence (9.7%), societal transformation (9.4%), entrepreneurship (8.7%), learning and growth (8.4%), creative expression (5.6%). Follow-up probing often revealed productivity asks were really quality-of-life asks — automating email became time with family. Roughly a third of visions are about making room for life, a quarter about better work, a fifth about becoming someone better. Societal-transformation hopes centred on healthcare (earlier cancer detection, drug discovery, access) and on education breaking the quality-wealth link in low/middle-income countries (source: 2026-07-16-What 81,000 people want from AI.md).

## Are they getting it?

81% said AI had taken a step toward their vision. Delivery clusters: productivity (32%), AI hasn't delivered (18.9%), cognitive partnership (17.2%), learning (9.9%), technical accessibility (8.7%), research synthesis (7.2%), emotional support (6.1%). The accessibility stories emphasise access over speed — a mute Ukrainian user building a text-to-speech bot, a Chilean butcher turned app entrepreneur; in collapsed-support contexts (Ukrainian war zones, bereavement) AI fills gaps human institutions no longer cover, with acknowledged downsides ("I should have talked with that friend, not you. That's how I lost that friend") (source: 2026-07-16-What 81,000 people want from AI.md).

## What people worry about

Thirteen concern categories (multi-label, mean 2.3 per respondent): unreliability (26.7%), jobs & economy (22.3%), autonomy & agency (21.9%), cognitive atrophy (16.3%), governance (14.7%), misinformation (13.6%), surveillance & privacy (13.1%), malicious use (13.0%), meaning & creativity (11.7%), overrestriction (11.7%), wellbeing & dependency (11.2%), sycophancy (10.8%), existential risk (6.7%). Jobs-and-economy concern was the strongest predictor of overall sentiment; ~11% expressed no concern (source: 2026-07-16-What 81,000 people want from AI.md).

## Light and shade

Five recurring tensions where benefit and harm entangle — learning vs cognitive atrophy, decision-making vs unreliability, emotional support vs dependence, time-saving vs illusory productivity, economic empowerment vs displacement. Benefits are generally experience-grounded while harms lean hypothetical, except decision-making vs unreliability — the only tension where the negative (37%) overshadows the positive (22%), with both sides deeply lived, especially in law, finance, government and healthcare. Tensions co-occur within the same person: those valuing emotional support are three times likelier to fear dependence. Educators report witnessing cognitive atrophy at 2.5-3x the average rate (presumably in students), while tradespeople show high learning benefit (45%) and almost no atrophy — suggesting AI-for-learning works best when volitional. Economic empowerment skews heavily to independent workers (47% vs 14% of institutional employees; 58% for employees with side projects); freelance creatives are the exposed middle where upside (23%) and precarity (17%) nearly cancel (source: 2026-07-16-What 81,000 people want from AI.md).

## Key Takeaways

- People are organised around values (security, learning, connection), not optimist/pessimist camps — hope and fear about the same capability coexist in the same person.
- The economic tension is the most speculative and the most sentiment-defining: displacement worry is evenly spread, but realised gains concentrate among independent workers.
- Unreliability is the lone domain where lived harm outweighs lived benefit — the verification burden is the day-to-day tax of current AI.
- Methodologically, AI-interviewer-at-scale (80K conversational interviews, classifier-coded) is itself the headline: qualitative depth at census breadth.
