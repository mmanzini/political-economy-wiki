# Enterprise AI adoption gap — reading the Xebia/Data Expo Monitor 2025/26

**Source:** [data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md](../../../Resources/documents/xebia/data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md)
**Underlying report:** Data & AI Monitor 2025/26 (Xebia + Data Expo)
**Assessment date:** 2026-06-04

---

## Summary

A critical read of Xebia and Data Expo's Data & AI Monitor 2025/26, a 500+ respondent survey of (mostly Central-European, US, MEA) data professionals. The directional story — adoption outpacing governance, budget, and skills, plus strong European unease about US Big-Tech dependence — is well corroborated by harder independent research. The specific positive percentages are soft: a self-selected, AI-enthusiast, management-heavy sample measuring perceptions, not outcomes, with the publisher commercially invested in the "ambition vs delivery gap" narrative.

## What the report is, and its two structural caveats

Published by Xebia (an IT consultancy selling AI services) with the Data Expo conference, surveying 500+ professionals recruited largely through their own event and network (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md). Two caveats: the publisher has a commercial interest in the gap narrative, and the sample is self-selected data professionals — the enthusiast end of the market, not the market. Eurostat finds only ~20% of EU enterprises use AI at all (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md).

## Key findings reported

- Only 33% agree their organisation is "fully harvesting and tracking the value AI brings" (8% strongly, 25% agree); 67% neutral or disagree (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md)
- 51% say their organisation knows where and how it wants to apply AI; only 28% trust AI output as much as a colleague's judgment (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md)
- Top barriers: data quality (51%), lack of data-driven culture (46%), data access (31%), budget and know-how (30% each) (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md)
- 72% rate themselves dependent on third-party vendors; 52% say American Big Tech holds too much power; 65% totally agree Europe should form a stronger front (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md)
- Experimentation-budget gap: 75–80% of leaders say there is "room" to experiment, only 55–60% say budget exists, technical teams see 35–40% (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md)
- ~50% believe competitors are further along; fewer than 40% have automated data ingestion (dashboards 80%+ are the only near-universal capability) (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md)

## Data or perception?

Almost nothing in the report is measured data — every figure is a Likert-scale perception statement, with no telemetry, financial data, or audited outcomes (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md). Harder independent evidence lands much lower:

- MIT GenAI Divide (2025): ~95% of GenAI pilots deliver no measurable P&L impact (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md)
- McKinsey State of AI: only 39% attribute any EBIT impact, mostly under 5% of EBIT; ~6% qualify as genuine high performers (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md)
- BCG (Sept 2025): 5% of companies are "future-built" for AI (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md)
- Gartner (June 2025): predicts over 40% of agentic AI projects cancelled by end of 2027 (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md)

The 33% "fully harvesting" figure is internally contradictory — rigorous value tracking needs measurement infrastructure most respondents admit they lack; a realistic figure for organisations rigorously tracking AI value is probably 5–15% (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md).

## FOMO, social desirability, and internal quality issues

Self-reported AI-adoption estimates can disagree by up to 40 percentage points in the same setting (Ling, Kale & Imas, SSRN; flagged by a Fed note); in corporate contexts the bias runs toward "AI washing" — overreporting to appear savvy — and this sample is ~45% management/C-suite, the most exposed group. The tell shows internally: leaders rate readiness higher than the technical teams doing the work (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md). Report quality issues compound it: Figures 6 and 8 share identical numbers (likely copy-paste), Figures 2 and 12 too; the exec summary and body disagree on vendor-dependence comfort; there is no methodology section (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md).

## How to read it

Treat pessimistic findings as more credible than optimistic ones: admissions of weakness (poor data quality, no budget, low trust in AI output) have no incentive to fake and match MIT, McKinsey, Gartner, and BCG. Claims of success ("fully harvesting value", "clear strategy") should be discounted heavily — biased sample, perception not measurement, social desirability pushing upward (source: data-ai-monitor-2025-26-critical-assessment.md).

## Key Takeaways

- Directional story (adoption outpacing governance/budget/skills; European Big-Tech unease) is well corroborated; the positive percentages are soft
- Every figure is perception, not measurement; independent hard-evidence research (MIT 95% no-impact, McKinsey, BCG 5%, Gartner 40%-cancelled) lands far lower
- Realistic "rigorously tracking AI value" figure ≈ 5–15%, not the reported 33%
- Sample is enthusiast + management-heavy → social-desirability "AI washing" bias; trust the admissions of weakness, discount the claims of success
