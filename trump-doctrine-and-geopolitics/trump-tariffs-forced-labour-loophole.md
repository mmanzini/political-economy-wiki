# Trump's tariff return via the forced-labour loophole

**Source:** [Il Post US newsletter, 25 July 2026](https://www.ilpost.it/)
**Author:** Il Post (weekly US-politics newsletter)

---

## Summary

Tariffs are back. After the Supreme Court ruled Trump could not impose tariffs bypassing Congress — with the White House still refunding tens of billions collected illegally — the administration found a new legal doorway: a forced-labour trade statute used against China in Trump's first term. New duties now cover over eighty countries accounting for 99.4% of US imports. The newsletter also covers the EU's record Google fine, the permanent daylight-saving-time push, and the re-escalating Iran war (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

## The mechanism

- The invoked statute allows tariffs against countries with trade practices harmful to the US linked to forced labour. The novel theory: most countries are not accused of exploiting workers themselves, but of failing to block goods made with exploited labour from entering their markets and flowing on to the US — e.g. a Dutch firm using Chinese components made by exploited workers (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).
- Two rates: 10% for countries with an (imperfectly enforced or merely promised) import-ban law — EU, Canada, Mexico, India, Taiwan, Malaysia, Indonesia; 12.5% for the rest, including China, Hong Kong, Australia, Brazil, South Korea, Singapore. Per the newsletter, nobody is deemed compliant (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).
- Crucially, the statute requires no external verification of the accusations — no official review of their merits, only an investigation and hearings, which were held. Unlike the previous round, no Supreme Court rescue is expected; the first-term China tariffs under this norm survived every legal challenge (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).
- Steel, aluminium and autos are excluded (already under heavier tariffs), other goods are covered by negotiated deals; experts expect modest overall economic effects since the new duties resemble the temporary post-ruling tariffs just expiring. Separately, Canada was hit with 50% duties on a list including wine, hockey sticks and cement under yet another statute; Brazil's 12.5% stacks on an existing 25% imposed for reasons from anticorruption failures to deforestation and treatment of US digital firms (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

## Protectionism as ratchet

The Commission meanwhile fined Google €890M under the Digital Markets Act — the largest DMA fine yet, in a near-monthly cadence of penalties against US tech that has angered Washington. Josh Lipsky's argument in the NYT: with an enormous public debt and tariff revenues potentially reaching hundreds of billions a year already committed to new spending, whoever wins in 2028 will struggle to dismantle the tariffs — the US "will be protectionist for a good while". The newsletter counters that the twentieth century's tariff dismantling happened precisely because removing them demonstrably multiplied trade and wealth (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

## Permanent daylight saving time, again

The House passed the Sunshine Protection Act making DST year-round, with broad bipartisan support and Trump personally phoning senators. The US tried this before: Nixon's 1973 Emergency DST Act during the oil crisis was repealed after eight months when approval collapsed from 79% to 42% as Americans woke in darkness. Senate holdouts (notably Tom Cotton) and sleep-medicine bodies — which favour permanent standard time as better aligned with circadian rhythms — stand opposed; with permanent DST, sunrise would come as late as 8:25 in New York and 9:01 in Montana (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

## In brief

- Iran: renewed US bombardments and Iranian retaliation (three US soldiers killed, nearly a hundred wounded in two weeks), Houthi involvement, oil back above $100/barrel; Trump meanwhile granted Saudi Arabia a generous civil-nuclear deal with light oversight; a detained US woman's release hints at back-channel dialogue (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).
- US politics: socialist candidates lost Arizona Democratic primaries; the DNC moved its first presidential primary to South Carolina; ICE made 16,213 arrests in the first ten days of July (1,400+/day) under new DHS secretary Markwayne Mullin; ~6,000 non-citizens were mistakenly added to New Jersey voter rolls by a DMV software error (fewer than 400 voted); measles cases hit a 35-year high; NYC mayor Mamdani walked back his Netanyahu-arrest promise with applause-winning theatre; JD Vance's fourth child was born — the first sitting VP birth since 1870 (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

## Key Takeaways

- The forced-labour statute gives Trump a verification-free, court-tested tariff channel — the obsession found a door, and this time no SCOTUS backstop applies.
- Fiscal lock-in is the deeper story: tariff revenue is already spent, making protectionism structurally sticky beyond 2028 regardless of who wins.
- The near-universal coverage (99.4% of imports) with near-identical rates exposes the labour rationale as pretext; a secondary aim is pushing third countries to cut imports from China.
- The Iran war has fully reignited, with oil above $100 and Saudi nuclear concessions redrawing Gulf alignments.
