# European politics digest — 25 June 2026 (Ventisette)

**Source:** [Ventisette — Il Post EU newsletter, 25 June 2026](https://www.ilpost.it/)
**Author:** Il Post (*Ventisette*, weekly Thursday EU newsletter)

---

## Summary

A six-item European roundup: the stalled UK-EU rapprochement ten years after Brexit, the digital euro's first parliamentary approval, France's record heatwave, the German Linke's surge under a new leadership, the Taliban's first Brussels meeting, and a looming discipline decision in the European Parliament (source: 2026-06-25-Gmail - Bregret.md).

## The slow UK-EU reset and "Bregret"

Ten years after the Brexit referendum, neither the British economy nor its politics is better off. Estimates put 2025 GDP 6–8% below where it would have been without Brexit, and politics has grown more unstable and fragmented, partly through the rise of Nigel Farage's Reform UK. A clear majority now think leaving the EU was a mistake (YouGov) — the regret captured by the portmanteau "Bregret". Labour won the 2024 election partly promising rapprochement, but progress is slow: a cooperation deal on Ukraine and counter-terrorism and UK participation in Erasmus from 2027 are agreed, but a July meeting was cancelled, and PM Keir Starmer resigned after a prolonged leadership crisis. His likely successor is the pro-European Andy Burnham, who recently took a parliamentary seat as a precondition for the leadership (source: 2026-06-25-Gmail - Bregret.md).

## The digital euro

The European Parliament gave a first approval to the regulation creating the digital euro, a digital-payment alternative to physical cash. A central rationale is reducing dependence on US firms: in 2022, 61% of all card payments inside the EU ran through foreign (mainly Visa and Mastercard) circuits, and 13 countries use only foreign systems, exposing the bloc to US leverage — with precedents in the post-2022 sanctions that disabled Visa/Mastercard in Russia and hit sanctioned individuals such as ICC judges and UN rapporteur Francesca Albanese. The Commission proposed the regulation in 2023; the Council gave first approval in December; a July plenary vote then opens trilogue with the Council, and the ECB expects the digital euro operational in 2029 (source: 2026-06-25-Gmail - Bregret.md).

## France and the heatwave

Much of Europe is very hot, and France is among the worst hit: 24 June was the hottest day ever recorded, 1,800 schools closed, and 40 people drowned since 18 June while swimming in unsupervised areas to cool off. On 25 June, 72 of 96 departments were placed on red alert — the highest number ever. Europe is warming faster than any other continent, and an estimated 50% of French buildings are insufficiently insulated against heat (source: 2026-06-25-Gmail - Bregret.md).

## The German Linke

Since 20 June, the German left party Linke has two new co-presidents: Ines Schwerdtner (reconfirmed) and Luigi Pantisano, its first president of foreign origin — the son of Italian *Gastarbeiter*. Polling around 11%, the Linke has doubled its membership in two years and is now the most-voted party among under-24s, helped by its pro-welfare, anti-rent-hike positions and its standing as one of Germany's most pro-Palestinian parties (source: 2026-06-25-Gmail - Bregret.md).

## The Taliban in Brussels

On 23 June a Taliban delegation met, for the first time, representatives of the European Commission and 15 member states in Brussels, to discuss repatriating Afghan nationals who have committed crimes in the EU or are deemed dangerous. The meeting was heavily criticised for lending legitimacy to a regime that systematically violates human rights and brutally discriminates against women, and because Afghanistan is not a safe country for returns. Afghans were the second-largest asylum-seeking nationality in the EU in 2025 (63,830, after Venezuela's 89,455) (source: 2026-06-25-Gmail - Bregret.md).

## EU Parliament discipline

Parliament president Roberta Metsola must decide whether to sanction two far-right MEPs — Danish Kristoffer Storm and Finnish Sebastian Tynkkynen — for racist conduct toward Swedish-Iraqi MEP Abir Al-Sahlani after the 17 June vote on the new EU returns regulation (which allows detention centres for foreigners outside the EU), when far-right members chanted "send them back". Sanctions can range from a warning to loss of the daily subsistence allowance (€359) up to a 60-day suspension; a recent precedent was a 30-day suspension of Polish far-right MEP Grzegorz Braun. Briefly noted elsewhere: a Czech public-broadcaster strike over funding changes, Romania without a PM for nearly two months, criticism of European Council president António Costa for phoning Putin's aides, and continuing anti-government protests in Albania (source: 2026-06-25-Gmail - Bregret.md).

## Key Takeaways

- Ten years on, UK opinion has shifted to majority "Bregret" with GDP an estimated 6–8% below the no-Brexit counterfactual; a pro-European Labour leadership (likely Burnham) inherits a slow reset.
- The digital euro cleared a first EP approval, framed explicitly as European payment sovereignty against US card networks; ECB targets 2029.
- France's June heatwave set records (hottest day, 72/96 departments on red alert), exposing poor building insulation across the continent warming fastest.
- The EU's first formal Taliban meeting (over Afghan repatriations) and a pending MEP-discipline decision both surface the bloc's hard-edged 2026 migration politics.
