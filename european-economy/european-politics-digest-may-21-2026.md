## Summary

A digest of European political stories from the 21 May 2026 issue of Ventisette: Trump's Greenland negotiations advancing with US military and veto demands; EU institutions reaching a compromise to ratify the controversial July 2025 US-EU trade deal under threat of new 25% auto tariffs; the rise of a digital-populist party in Cyprus (Fidias Panayiotou's Democrazia Diretta); Eurostat employment data showing Italy at the EU's lowest female-employment rate; an Irish gangster running again for parliament.

## Body

### Greenland — Trump's quiet pressure campaign

Despite focus on the Middle East war and Cuba threats, Trump has not given up on Greenland (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). A *New York Times* investigation revealed US demands in secret negotiations with Denmark and Greenland: expanded indefinite military presence and **veto rights over major investments on the island** (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). Trump's special envoy for Greenland, Louisiana Governor Jeff Landry, visited the island and drew criticism — including a viral clip promising Greenlandic children "all the chocolate cookies they want" in Louisiana, an ill-judged framing given Greenland's colonial sensitivity to Danish-era abuses (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). The US fear: an independent Greenland would be more exposed to Russian and Chinese influence (cf. 2018 pressure against a Chinese airport-expansion contract in Nuuk and Ilulissat, ultimately won by a Danish-backed firm) (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

### EU-US trade deal — the compromise to ratify

European Commission, Parliament, and Council representatives reached a compromise allowing the **controversial July 2025 US-EU trade deal** to be approved and enter force (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). The original deal was widely criticised as a capitulation: 15% US tariffs on many European products to avoid worse, and EU commitment to zero its own tariffs on a range of US imports (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

Parliament had stalled the deal to insert protection clauses — including one that would have voided the agreement if the US threatened EU territorial integrity (as had occurred with Greenland) — but many member governments opposed the clauses, fearing they would "irritate Trump" (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). The compromise dropped the strongest Parliament proposals. Trump threatened new 25% tariffs on EU machines and trucks if the deal doesn't enter force by 4 July; the US ambassador to the EU published a Politico op-ed attacking Brussels delays (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

### Cyprus — Fidias and digital populism

Cyprus holds parliamentary elections Sunday. A new party — **Democrazia Diretta di Cipro** — is likely to enter parliament, polling around 6% (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). Its founder is YouTuber and MEP **Fidias Panayiotou**, originally elected on an "apolitical" platform but having drifted toward increasingly **pro-Russian positions** (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). Fidias is running for the Cypriot parliament without resigning his MEP seat (incompatible roles); he says he'll let his followers vote on which to keep. Democrazia Diretta has no real programme; candidates were selected via an app called **Agorà** that lets registered users vote on parliamentary positions — structurally reminiscent of early Movimento 5 Stelle (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

### Eurostat — EU employment at post-2009 high, Italy at the bottom

Eurostat 2025 data: EU employment rate at **76.1%**, the highest since 2009 (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). Top: Malta 83.6%, Netherlands 83.4%, Czech Republic 82.9%. Bottom: Greece 71%, Romania 69%, **Italy 67.6%** (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). Italy's rate is at its all-time high — a fact the Meloni government regularly cites — but remains well below EU average. Italy has the **lowest female employment rate in the EU (58%)** and the **largest gender employment gap (19.1 percentage points)** (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). Lithuania is the only EU country where female employment exceeds male (80.3% vs 79.7%).

### Ireland — Gerard Hutch's second run

Gerard Hutch, 63, considered by Irish justice authorities the head of a powerful criminal organisation, is making a second attempt to enter parliament in a Dublin by-election (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). Hutch served roughly a decade in prison for burglary, receiving stolen goods, and assault, but was acquitted on the most serious charges — eligible to run. He came very close in 2024. His platform centres on housing crisis, inflation, and immigration, from right-wing populist positions (source: 2026-05-21-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

## Key Takeaways

- US Greenland demands are concrete and far-reaching: indefinite military presence plus veto over major investments — a sovereignty constraint Greenland calls unacceptable but cannot effectively resist.
- The EU-US trade deal compromise required dropping protection clauses tied to territorial-integrity threats; member governments prioritised avoiding Trump's anger over institutional autonomy.
- Digital-populist parties continue to penetrate small EU member states (Cyprus following the Italian M5S playbook), now drifting toward pro-Russian positioning.
- Italy's record employment hides the EU's largest gender gap; the "all-time-high" framing obscures structural failure on female and youth employment.
- Eligible-but-criminal candidates (Hutch) continue testing the boundary between political populism and organised crime.
