## Summary

Il Post's Italian-language newsletter of 14 May 2026 covers six interlinked European stories: the Labour Party crisis under Keir Starmer after local-election disaster; EU aviation guidance as the Strait of Hormuz enters its third month of closure; the inauguration of Hungary's new Péter Magyar government; the EU's emergency civil-protection mechanism (UCPM/RescEU); the Andalusia regional elections; and Norway's ongoing fishing cooperation with Russia despite EU concerns.

## UK Labour and Starmer's survival

Labour suffered a catastrophic result in English local elections and in Scottish and Welsh parliamentary votes, with a significant faction calling for Starmer's resignation (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). Starmer says he will stay, citing the risk of "chaos" without him. Two most-cited succession candidates: Manchester mayor Andy Burnham (needs a Labour MP to resign for a by-election he would then need to win — complex) and Health Secretary Wes Streeting (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

The crisis is attributed to several scandals, Starmer's subdued style, and a gap between 2024 campaign promises and government delivery. Politically, the 2026 elections are described as a historic fragmentation: Labour and Conservatives no longer dominate as they did for a century, with the Greens growing among young/left voters and Reform UK on the right (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

## Hormuz closure and EU aviation

The Strait of Hormuz has been closed for nearly three months; the EU Commission has issued guidance on how existing 2004 transport regulations apply to cancelled flights. Key points (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md):

- If a European flight is cancelled, passengers have the right to a refund or rebooking to the same destination.
- If cancelled with fewer than 14 days' notice, compensation of €250–600 is owed — *except* when the cancellation is due to fuel shortage, which qualifies as extraordinary circumstances.
- Aviation fuel for now remains sufficient; EU member-state imports from the US have increased as a partial substitute for Gulf-sourced fuel.
- The Commission warns the situation could worsen if Hormuz is not reopened within weeks.

## Hungary's new government

Péter Magyar's cabinet took office on Saturday (10 May 2026, five weeks after his April 12 supermajority election win). The government positions itself as pro-European and more liberal than Orbán's 16-year administration. Priority agenda: anti-corruption reform and dismantling the power structures Orbán built (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

Notable cabinet appointment: Vilmos Kátai-Németh as Minister for Social and Family Policy — a blind MP (first in Hungarian history), Aikidō black belt, who ran irreverent viral social-media campaigns during the election (including racing a slow train to demonstrate infrastructure decay) (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

## EU emergency mechanisms — UCPM and RescEU

Prompted by the Hondius cruise-ship hantavirus outbreak (passengers evacuated at Tenerife, Monday): EU health emergency response operates mainly through national governments, but the Commission plays a coordinating role via the **Union Civil Protection Mechanism (UCPM)**, established 2001, invoked more than 830 times since then (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

UCPM structure: a member state in a disaster can request EU assistance; the Commission coordinates aid from other member states and contributes from its own reserve, called **RescEU**. The Commission also has the **European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC)** for monitoring infectious disease. Italy used UCPM for the 2023 Emilia-Romagna floods (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

In the Hondius case, the Commission helped plan and fund repatriation flights and sent an air ambulance with specialists (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

## Andalusia regional elections

Elections on Sunday in Andalusia (Spain's most populous region). Polls point strongly to the PP (Partito Popolare, centre-right), which holds the current absolute majority and may retain it without needing Vox as a coalition partner — though Vox continues to grow (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

Historical context: Andalusia was the "granary" of Spanish socialism for decades; since around 2018 it has shifted significantly rightward, with Vox winning its first Spanish parliamentary seats there (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). PSOE candidate María Jesús Montero (MP and physician; former minister) has campaigned on Andalusia's public-health deterioration; former PM Zapatero has campaigned alongside her to energise left-wing voters. The election is also read as a barometer of PM Pedro Sánchez's national popularity, which has suffered from scandals including an investigation involving his wife Begoña Gómez (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

## Norway–Russia fishing agreement

Despite post-Ukraine sanctions and diplomatic freeze, Norway continues cooperating with Russia in one narrow domain: cod fishing in the Barents Sea, governed by a 1975 bilateral agreement that gives Russia access to Norwegian ports and waters (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

The EU has expressed concern that Russia uses this access for espionage of sensitive infrastructure. EU Fisheries Commissioner Costas Kadis has repeatedly asked Norway to revise the agreements (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). In 2025 Norway banned two Russian shipping companies (Norebo and Murman) — both EU-sanctioned for suspected data collection on sensitive infrastructure — from ports and territorial waters; Russia complained but no significant consequences followed, and general fishing cooperation continues (source: 2026-05-14-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).
