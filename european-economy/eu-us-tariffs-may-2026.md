## Summary

A digest of five interconnected European political stories in May 2026: the EU-US trade deal stalemate threatening new tariffs, Keir Starmer's deepening unpopularity ahead of UK local/devolved elections, the planned US troop reduction in Germany, Lithuania's contested public broadcaster reform, and Canada's alignment with the EU as the "most European non-European country" — plus Eurovision/Israel protests.

## EU-US Trade Deal Stalemate

The EU-US trade deal struck in July 2025 has not yet been ratified: the European Parliament and the Council of the EU still need to vote. Trump threatened to increase auto/truck tariffs from 15% to 25% over delays. Germany (the largest auto exporter) is most exposed. The EU Parliament added protective clauses in March 2026; PPE now threatens to pass the deal without those clauses using far-right votes, risking a fracture of the centrist majority. (source: 2026-05-07-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md)

Context: the US Supreme Court annulled Trump's emergency-powers tariffs in February 2026; he approved new 10% provisional tariffs against everyone in response. (source: 2026-05-07-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md)

## UK: Another Rough Patch for Starmer

May 7 UK elections (Scottish/Welsh parliaments + English local councils) expected to go badly for Labour. Poor results could revive internal party calls for Starmer's resignation, though some fear that would increase confusion. (source: 2026-05-07-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md)

## US Troops in Germany

US to withdraw 5,000 military personnel from Germany — announced after German Chancellor Merz unusually criticised the US-Iran war. Germany hosts 36,436 US soldiers (by far the most in Europe); ~68,000 US soldiers stationed permanently across all of Europe. Functions include allied forces training, nuclear weapons maintenance, logistical hub for out-of-Europe operations, and Landstuhl military hospital (treating NATO allies and Middle East/Africa personnel). (source: 2026-05-07-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md)

## Lithuania: LRT Public Broadcaster Reform

Thousands of protesters opposing a government reform to LRT (Lithuanian public broadcaster) that would make it easier to replace the director — seen as increasing government control. The populist right-wing coalition partner "Alba del Nemunas" (in government since 2025 alongside Social Democrats) is driving the push. Lithuania had generally respected press freedom; protests are among the largest in national history. (source: 2026-05-07-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md)

## Canada as "Most European Non-European Country"

Canadian PM Mark Carney participated in the European Political Community meeting in Yerevan (Armenia) — first non-European leader invited. Since Trump's return, Canada has deepened EU ties (security/defence cooperation agreement, broader deal in progress on energy + AI). Carney called Canada "the most European country among non-European countries" and said the international order will be rebuilt "starting from Europe." (source: 2026-05-07-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md)

## Key Takeaways

- EU-US trade deal ratification is a political fault line: passing without protective clauses risks the centrist majority; stalling risks higher US auto tariffs targeting Germany (source: 2026-05-07-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md)
- US troop withdrawal from Germany after Merz's Iran-war criticism is a direct example of Trump using military presence as diplomatic leverage (source: 2026-05-07-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md)
- Canada's EU pivot is the most significant extra-European political alignment since Brexit; signals a new coalition of liberal democracies with Europe at the centre (source: 2026-05-07-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md)
- Lithuania's LRT reform follows a pattern of populist coalition partners using press/media law to test institutional resilience (source: 2026-05-07-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com.md)
