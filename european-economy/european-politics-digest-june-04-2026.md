## Summary

A Il Post Europa newsletter digest of early-June European political developments: Hungary's new Magyar/Tisza government hitting its first obstacle in a holdover Orbán-loyalist president; EU institutions tightening AI and digital-sovereignty posture; Armenia's pivotal pro-Western election under Russian pressure; a far-right "remigration" summit in Portugal; the quiet fading of Article 42.7 mutual-defence talk; and a Commission request to cull protected cormorants.

## Hungary: Magyar's first obstacle

Péter Magyar and his Tisza party have governed for nearly a month and face their first obstacle: President Tamás Sulyok, an Orbán/Fidesz loyalist, refuses to resign (source: 2026-06-04-Inbox (1) - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). The president has limited powers but signs and promulgates laws; Magyar fears Sulyok will block the reforms he says are needed to dismantle Orbán's 16-year authoritarian system. Magyar had given Sulyok until 31 May to step down; Sulyok, though very unpopular, refuses — his term runs to 2029 (source: 2026-06-04-Inbox (1) - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). Magyar threatens a constitutional reform to remove him (Tisza controls over two-thirds of parliament), but Sulyok and the Fidesz-appointed Constitutional Court could block it, risking a constitutional crisis (source: 2026-06-04-Inbox (1) - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). Meanwhile Magyar has agreed with the European Commission to unblock €16bn in frozen funds (13% of Hungarian GDP), conditional on passing anti-corruption and rule-of-law reforms by 31 August (source: 2026-06-04-Inbox (1) - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

## EU AI use and digital sovereignty

About 2,100 European Parliament staff — roughly one in five — use AI bots daily for their work, raising the risk of sharing confidential documents with (mostly US) private companies (source: 2026-06-04-Inbox (1) - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). The Parliament is therefore building its own EP GenAI Hub, expected operational this year. In a parallel sovereignty move the Commission presented measures to boost EU autonomy in semiconductors, AI, and cloud, and the Parliament dropped Google as its default search engine in favour of the French engine Qwant (source: 2026-06-04-Inbox (1) - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

## Armenia: Russia, Europe, and the 7 June election

Armenia holds parliamentary elections on 7 June; PM Nikol Pashinyan's pro-Western party is favoured to win (source: 2026-06-04-Inbox (1) - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). The US, EU, and Russia have all tried to influence the vote. Pashinyan seeks a peace deal with Azerbaijan (likely meaning definitive recognition of the loss of Nagorno-Karabakh, retaken in 2023) and better relations with Turkey — unpopular with Karabakh refugees now in Armenia (source: 2026-06-04-Inbox (1) - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). Russia, still hugely influential, suspended imports of Armenian agricultural products and, per Reuters citing intelligence reports, plans to bring tens of thousands of Russia-based Armenians to vote against Pashinyan; Putin has compared Armenia to Ukraine. The main pro-Russian party, Armenia Forte, is led by billionaire Samvel Karapetyan, who cannot run (no Armenian citizenship) and is on trial for allegedly inciting a 2025 coup (source: 2026-06-04-Inbox (1) - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

## Far-right "remigration" summit and other items

Gregory Bovino — former US Border Patrol head, known for brutal anti-immigration operations and a coat compared to Nazi-era military dress — attended a Portugal summit on "remigration," the far-right idea of forcibly expelling foreigners (source: 2026-06-04-Inbox (1) - manzini.m@gmail.com.md). Attendees included AfD and Spanish Vox politicians; Italy's Roberto Vannacci sent a message of support; Bovino said he was ready to help European governments expel immigrants, citing Germany where AfD polls first (source: 2026-06-04-Inbox (1) - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

**Article 42.7.** The EU treaty's mutual-assistance clause, much-discussed after Trump's NATO threats as an alternative guarantee (covering even non-NATO members Austria, Cyprus, Ireland, Malta), has quietly faded from EU priorities; von der Leyen had pushed to "bring the mutual-defence clause to life" and members ran May simulations, but Kaja Kallas says it is not being concretely discussed, and incoming Council presidency Ireland calls it a non-priority and unpopular (source: 2026-06-04-Inbox (1) - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

**Cormorants.** Several Central/Eastern European governments asked the Commission to allow hunting the common cormorant, protected under the 1979 Birds Directive, claiming it eats too much fish and harms the fishing sector; environmental groups object and the Commission has not decided (source: 2026-06-04-Inbox (1) - manzini.m@gmail.com.md).

## Key Takeaways

- Hungary: Magyar/Tisza supermajority blocked by a holdover Fidesz president and Constitutional Court — a possible constitutional crisis; €16bn EU funds unblocked but conditional on rule-of-law reforms by 31 Aug
- EU digital sovereignty hardening: ~1-in-5 EP staff use AI daily → EP GenAI Hub being built; Commission autonomy package on chips/AI/cloud; Parliament swaps Google for Qwant
- Armenia's 7 June election is a Russia-vs-West contest; Pashinyan favoured despite Russian economic pressure and alleged voter-importing
- Article 42.7 mutual-defence enthusiasm has cooled into a non-priority; far-right "remigration" gains transatlantic networking
