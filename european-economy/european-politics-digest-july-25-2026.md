# European politics digest — 25 July 2026

**Source:** [Il Post EU newsletter, 25 July 2026](https://www.ilpost.it/)
**Author:** Il Post (weekly European-politics newsletter)

---

## Summary

A European roundup: Andy Burnham becomes UK prime minister with a spending agenda and no money, the Commission proposes weakening the ETS, an unusual agricultural anti-subsidy probe against Chinese Peking-duck meat, a US State Department grant programme promoting a "civilisational bond" with Europe, Raffaele Fitto's new Cyprus role, Vienna's deliberately tilted Lueger statue, and Europe's bathing-water report (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com 1.md).

## Burnham's money problem

Andy Burnham (Labour) became UK prime minister on Monday 20 July, replacing the unpopular Keir Starmer and leaning hard on cost-of-living measures. His core problem is funding: he wants to honour Starmer's spending commitments while Starmer had also promised no tax rises. An aide warned of the need to avoid "a Liz Truss-style moment" with markets "waiting to be spooked" — and Truss herself predicted Burnham will cause a financial crisis. His first move: a VAT cut on household electricity, funded by scrapping Starmer's confused and unpopular digital-ID programme. Pro-European but quiet on foreign policy so far, Burnham is expected to continue the EU rapprochement, including youth-mobility and trade talks (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com 1.md).

## A weaker ETS

The EU has cut greenhouse emissions ~40% since the 1990s, largely credited to the Emissions Trading System. On Friday the Commission presented a plan to loosen its caps — framed as helping companies compete, but effectively weakening one of the Union's flagship environmental policies, after pressure from several governments including Italy's. The proposal still needs Council and Parliament approval (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com 1.md).

## The Peking-duck probe

On 9 July the Commission opened a rare agricultural anti-subsidy investigation against China over Peking-duck meat, after European producers accused Beijing of subsidising producers and cheap feed, enabling underpriced exports. Confirmed unfair competition could lead to tariffs — and possible Chinese retaliation against European products (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com 1.md).

## The US "civilisational bond" grants

On 13 July the US State Department published a funding call for organisations doing cultural and research work in Europe on migration, national sovereignty and religious freedom, aiming to strengthen the "bond between civilisations" — an idea the newsletter reads as resting on a racist vision of Europe and the US as white, Christian regions threatened by immigration. It extends the Trump administration's pattern of supporting European far-right movements. Relatedly, Elon Musk's endorsement of Marine Le Pen has embarrassed RN, given Trump's unpopularity even among the European far right (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com 1.md).

## Fitto, the invisible commissioner, gets Cyprus

The Commission named Italian commissioner Raffaele Fitto special representative for Cyprus, joining UN-led reunification talks for the island divided since the 1970s. Fitto keeps his main role as Cohesion and Reforms commissioner — nominally one of six vice-presidencies with a major portfolio, in practice with limited power under von der Leyen's centralised Commission; Euractiv dubbed him "the invisible commissioner" (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com 1.md).

## In brief

Vienna chose to keep the statue of antisemitic fin-de-siècle mayor Karl Lueger but tilt it several degrees as a deliberate visual disturbance, part of Austria's unresolved reckoning with Nazi-linked monuments (Hitler's birthplace is now a police station). The European Environment Agency's bathing-water report (22,000+ sites) finds 84.8% of waters "excellent" — Cyprus (100%), Greece (97.1%), Bulgaria (96.9%) top; Albania worst (16.8%); Italy above average (89.8%). Magyar offered Judit Polgár the Hungarian presidency; she declined (source: 2026-07-25-Inbox - manzini.m@gmail.com 1.md).

## Key Takeaways

- Burnham inherits Starmer's fiscal bind — spend more, tax no more — with bond markets primed for a Truss rerun; scrapping digital ID doubles as both funding source and civil-liberties signal.
- The ETS weakening marks a substantive retreat on EU climate policy under competitiveness pressure led partly by Italy.
- The duck probe is small-bore but signals the EU extending its anti-subsidy toolkit from EVs into agriculture, with retaliation risk.
- The State Department's "civilisational bond" grants institutionalise US funding of European identitarian politics.
