# Julie Meyer — from dot-com queen to serial fraud allegations (Dotcom)

**Source:** [Fuori da qui, Ep. 140 "Dotcom"](https://podcasttranscript.ai/library/ep-140-dotcom)
**Author:** Simone Pieranni (Cora Media podcast), building on Guardian and Malta Independent investigations

---

## Summary

The episode reconstructs the parabola of Julie Meyer, the American entrepreneur who arrived in London in 1998 with the dot-com boom, built First Tuesday into the networking hub of European startup finance, was celebrated as a "leader of tomorrow" and awarded an MBE — and whose later ventures (Ariadne Capital and successors) left a trail of unpaid debts, fraud allegations, and a fugitive exit from Malta with a princess crown in a suitcase. A UK judge called her a selfish and unreliable person whose word counts for nothing when it suits her to bend it (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Dotcom.md).

## First Tuesday and the bubble

- In October 1998 the first First Tuesday evening matched founders (green badge) with investors (red badge) in a Soho bar; within a year the monthly event drew sponsors like Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley and McKinsey and became a franchise credited with seeding much of the era's dot-com investment. The idea originally came from lastminute.com co-founder Brent Hoberman, who handed it to Meyer to run; her role was pure intermediation — commission without capital at risk (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Dotcom.md).
- When the bubble burst in 2000, her co-founders outvoted her and sold to an Israeli buyer for $50M in cash and shares; Meyer, who wanted an IPO, would recast the episode for years as men underestimating and liquidating her (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Dotcom.md).

## Ariadne Capital and the fund findings

- With her exit money Meyer founded Ariadne Capital ("entrepreneurs backing entrepreneurs", 62 entrepreneur-shareholders), built a lavish public persona — Trafalgar Square offices, driver, personal staff — described by an ex-employee as "a temple dedicated to Julie" run on fear (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Dotcom.md).
- The 2009 Ace Fund promised £60M; a 2011 investor report showed only ~£7.6M actually invested — and more than half of it (~£4M) spent buying 100% of one of Meyer's own companies. Investments mostly closed at a loss; unpaid contractors (e.g. consultant Rachel Lowe, awarded ~£26,000 by a court) multiplied. In December 2017 Ariadne entered administration; administrators valued remaining assets at £2,500 against hundreds of thousands owed to staff and the taxman (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Dotcom.md).
- A liquidator's report on her Tagstar vehicle described a three-container mechanism — investors paid into Tagstar for shares in DMT, money moved on to Ariadne, shares never issued — using the words fund diversion and fraud; Meyer inverted the accusation, claiming DMT defrauded her (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Dotcom.md).

## Malta, Zurich, Greece

- In summer 2017 Meyer moved to Malta, bought a licensed investment firm, and announced a €1B European fund with a summit attended by Malta's prime minister — while invoices already went unpaid (event designer Mark Lightfoot was left ~€60,000 short and dropped legal action after threats). In November 2017, with a hotel bill outstanding, she directed assistants by email to discreetly pack her valuables — including "the princess crown in the safe" — and leave the island; Malta's regulator suspended the fund licence and a magistrate ordered police to locate her within 48 hours (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Dotcom.md).
- In Zurich she rebuilt as Viva Investment Partners with new partner René Eichenberger. South African founder Simon Davies (Scarab Tech, plastic-waste-to-fuel machines) says a promised $900K investment evaporated; his investors' $236K, parked with a Swiss law firm, was moved at Meyer's request to a Lithuanian account, and instead of a refund he received a CHF 162,000 invoice for unrequested services. UK police closed the case in January 2026 for insufficient evidence; the Guardian found two near-identical 2023 cases (a Middle East price-comparison site, a Barcelona gin maker) totalling over $200K (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Dotcom.md).
- The pattern per her ex-partner: "once unmasked in one country she finds new backers who believe in her and moves to the next jurisdiction" — most recently Greece, where a 2024 investor event sold as yacht-and-gala turned out to be dinghies and sandwiches. Warwick University revoked her honorary degree; the MBE was withdrawn in 2026. Confronted by Guardian journalists in Zurich, she answered: "I am not Julie Meyer" (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Dotcom.md).

## Key Takeaways

- Meyer's career is a study in intermediation as power: the commission-taking "point of passage" role built in 1998 was repeated unchanged for 25 years across four jurisdictions.
- Regulators repeatedly opened and closed cases (FCA probe closed 2023 for insufficient evidence; Maltese, Swiss, UK, US complaints stalled) — cross-border fund flows and jurisdictional hopping defeated enforcement even with documented liquidator findings.
- Reputation infrastructure (MBE, media titles, summits with prime ministers) functioned as collateral long after the balance sheets stopped supporting it.
