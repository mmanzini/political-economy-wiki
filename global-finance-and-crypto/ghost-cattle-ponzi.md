# The ghost-cattle Ponzi — Brian McLean and Rabo Agri Finance (Schemi)

**Source:** [Fuori da qui, Ep. 139 "Schemi"](https://podcasttranscript.ai/library/ep-139-schemi)
**Author:** Simone Pieranni (Cora Media podcast), drawing on a June 2026 Wall Street Journal reportage (Margot Patrick, Patrick Thomas)

---

## Summary

Brian McLean, a cattle dealer from Benton, Kentucky, ran a classic Ponzi scheme dressed as livestock trading: he promised investors ~30% annual returns from buying and reselling calves, paid old investors with new investors' money, and pledged as loan collateral a herd of ~60,000 head of which more than 50,000 did not exist. Rabo Agri Finance lent up to $45M despite internal analysts documenting the anomalies for years. The scheme collapsed in February 2023 when a bank inspector physically counted the cows for the first time in over four years; McLean took his own life the day before his planned arrest, and a court-appointed trustee is still hunting ~$170M that simply cannot be found (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Schemi.md).

## The mechanism

- The legitimate core: buy young calves at auction, hold ~3 months while they gain weight and immunity, resell for finishing. McLean funded it with investor capital on 90-120 day contracts promising ~30% a year; word of mouth spread the circle from Kentucky to Tennessee, Colorado and Canada, including a pharmacist ("Brown") who entrusted tens of millions at ~20% and a truck dealer (John Tindall) who got $650K back on $600K in months — and, like almost everyone, reinvested it all (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Schemi.md).
- Profits came from new investors, not cattle sales; the trustee later described it as a carousel that "stopped being a scheme about cattle and became a scheme about money" (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Schemi.md).

## Why cattle are terrible collateral

- Unlike a house, cattle move and look alike: McLean kept animals split between Kentucky and Texas, 1,500 km apart, so wherever anyone looked, "the bulk" was plausibly elsewhere; investors who flew out to check ear tags were counting their slice, which really existed (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Schemi.md).
- US agricultural finance still runs on generational trust and handshakes; paper trails (invoices, 24-hour payment rules) exist, but nobody reconciled the paper numbers with animals in pens for four years — McLean himself compiled the certificates the bank relied on (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Schemi.md).
- Precedent: Washington State's Cody Easterday billed Tyson Foods for 200,000 non-existent head ("ghost cattle", $244M) to cover commodity-trading losses, caught only when COVID supply-chain disruption prompted a physical count (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Schemi.md).

## The bank that documented its own blindness

- Rabo Agri Finance (US arm of the Dutch cooperative Rabobank lineage) initially rejected McLean in 2017 — he didn't brand his animals and couldn't prove ownership — then a loan officer rehabilitated his books and first lent ~$7M in 2018 (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Schemi.md).
- Internal chronology: October 2019, $2.5M overdraft ("what the hell"); December 2019, an internal report flags check-kiting-style fraud signals; late 2020, analysts list McLean as high-risk with feed costs impossibly low for the declared herd — answered by "if it's not broken, don't fix it"; February 2021, an analyst writes the sale prices "make no sense... we have no idea who owes what to whom"; August 2021, the credit line is raised to $45M. Lawsuits (contested by the bank, which calls itself a victim; some claims already rejected by a judge) allege commission income drove the escalation (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Schemi.md).

## Collapse

- In February 2023 an inspector counting cattle in Texas found ~8,900 of a documented ~60,000; a colleague's message: "the house of cards has collapsed". As investors demanded money back at once, McLean's checks bounced; police agreed to arrest him the next morning, and he died by suicide at 52, leaving a note reading "it all came down today". Balance sheets prepared for the bank were later found hidden under a tarp in a disused snake-infested barn (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Schemi.md).
- The trustee is clawing back investor "profits" as fraud proceeds (Brown says he ultimately lost $5M) and cannot locate ~$170M — no Cayman accounts, no visible lifestyle inflation, nothing (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Schemi.md).

## Key Takeaways

- Mobile, fungible collateral plus trust-based lending is a structural fraud surface: the fix (physical verification) was skipped for years precisely because relationships substituted for controls.
- The bank's own written record shows fraud awareness coexisting with credit expansion — an incentive problem (fee income), not an information problem.
- "Ghost cattle" is a repeatable pattern (Easterday/Tyson before McLean/Rabo); external shocks, not audits, tend to trigger the counting that ends these schemes.
