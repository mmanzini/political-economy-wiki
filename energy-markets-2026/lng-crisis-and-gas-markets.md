# LNG Crisis and Gas Markets

**Source:** Multiple press reports (Reuters, Bloomberg, IEA) — compiled April 2026
**Author:** (source: needs-verification)

---

## Summary

Qatar's Ras Laffan LNG facility — producing 93% of Qatar's LNG output — was attacked and partially shut down from 2026-03-02, sending Asian LNG prices up 127% in 30 days. With 90% of Hormuz LNG destined for Asian markets, the supply collapse has cascading effects on electricity, industry, and food production (via fertiliser).

## LNG Supply Collapse

- Qatar exports 93% of its LNG through Hormuz — production suspended 2026-03-02, force majeure declared 2026-03-04 (source: lng-crisis-and-gas-markets.md)
- Ras Laffan (93% of Qatar's LNG output) attacked — 17% capacity damaged, repair up to 5 years (source: lng-crisis-and-gas-markets.md)
- Work has resumed at Ras Laffan but full operations are far off (source: lng-crisis-and-gas-markets.md)
- Asian LNG prices surged **127% in 30 days** (source: lng-crisis-and-gas-markets.md)

## European Gas (TTF)

- TTF up 2.5% at €46.44/MWh
- Driven by Hormuz closure and supply uncertainty

## Hormuz Impact on Gas

- 90% of LNG transiting Hormuz is destined for Asian markets
- Physical scarcity, not just price — gas is needed for electricity, heating, chips manufacturing, and fertiliser production
- Without gas, the knock-on effects hit food supply (urea/fertiliser production)

## Key Takeaways

- Qatar's LNG force majeure is a direct threat to Japan, South Korea, and other Asian importers
- 127% price surge in 30 days — the most violent reaction among energy markets
- Damage to Ras Laffan may take years to repair, creating a structural gap
- Gas scarcity has cascading effects: energy → industry → food (via fertilisers)
