# 2001 and the rise of surveillance capitalism (Venticinque)

**Source:** [Fuori da qui, Ep. 141 "Venticinque"](https://podcasttranscript.ai/library/ep-141-venticinque)
**Author:** Simone Pieranni (Corriere/Cora Media podcast), with Tomaso Delorenzi

---

## Summary

Twenty-five years on, the podcast reads three events of 2001 — the Genoa G8 protests and Indymedia's open publishing, the 9/11 attacks and the surveillance state they legitimised, and China's WTO entry alongside the Golden Shield project — not as separate stories but as three scenes of one film: the end of the free-internet illusion and the rise of surveillance capitalism (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Venticinque.md).

## Genoa and Indymedia's open publishing

- During the July 2001 G8 in Genoa, police killed 23-year-old protester Carlo Giuliani and raided the Diaz school in a beating the European Court of Human Rights would later qualify as torture; hundreds of cameras — activists, freelancers, bystanders — documented events (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Venticinque.md).
- The footage flowed to Indymedia, born in 1999 around Matthew Arnison's Australian software and the Seattle WTO protests, on the principle of "open publishing": anyone could publish text, photo, video without permission — the slogan was "don't hate the media, become the media". Indymedia Italia's Diaz-Pascoli media centre was the nerve node during the Genoa days, and its footage helped overturn the official account of the Diaz raid through three levels of judicial review (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Venticinque.md).
- In October 2004 the FBI, acting on a Bologna prosecutor's request, seized two Indymedia servers in a London Rackspace data centre, knocking out 22 national Indymedia sites at once — a transatlantic show of force proving the network had an address, and an address can always be reached (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Venticinque.md).

## 9/11, TIA and Palantir

- After 9/11, the Patriot Act (26 October 2001, 342 pages) expanded FBI/intelligence surveillance powers; the Department of Homeland Security (November 2002) merged 22 federal agencies in the biggest federal reorganisation since 1947 (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Venticinque.md).
- At DARPA, admiral John Poindexter's Information Awareness Office built Total Information Awareness — a "Manhattan Project for counterespionage" designed to cross-correlate financial, travel and communications data; Congress defunded it in 2003, the podcast argues, less from ethics than because it was too visible — surveillance capitalism needs opacity (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Venticinque.md).
- Poindexter's idea re-emerged as a corporate variant: Palantir Technologies (2003, Peter Thiel and Alex Karp), which received a key early investment from In-Q-Tel, the CIA's venture arm, in 2005 — the archetype of the private company performing state intelligence functions, followed by contracts with NSA, FBI, CIA, the Pentagon and police forces worldwide. The true heirs of TIA, the podcast argues, are Google, Facebook, Amazon and Microsoft — surveillance camouflaged inside everyday services (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Venticinque.md).

## China: WTO entry and the Golden Shield

- In December 2001 China entered the WTO; in parallel Beijing was building the Golden Shield, the largest digital-surveillance system in the world — national video surveillance, digital ID cards, integrated databases — initially with Western suppliers: Cisco (routers allegedly customised to track Falun Gong, per an internal slide), Nortel (JungleMUX video surveillance), Sun Microsystems, Motorola; Yahoo handed dissidents' emails to the government (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Venticinque.md).
- Hikvision, founded in Hangzhou as a spin-off of a state military-electronics research institute (CETC), became the world's largest video-surveillance maker, present in over 150 countries by 2017 — until the US blacklisted it in October 2019 over Xinjiang surveillance, a move the podcast calls hypocritical given the same US firms had built China's control apparatus and the NSA's PRISM/XKeyscore intercept millions worldwide (source: 2026-07-25-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Venticinque.md).

## Key Takeaways

- The 2001 conjuncture — Genoa, 9/11, China's WTO entry — marks the pivot from the open-web promise to surveillance as both governance norm and business model.
- Palantir and Hikvision are two faces of the same phenomenon: West and China each built a public-private surveillance hybrid, with the West supplying China's early architecture.
- The Indymedia server seizure prefigured today's platform reality: distributed, horizontal information can be silenced through infrastructure chokepoints.
- The podcast's closing move is Mark Fisher-inflected: rather than only asking how to defend ourselves, ask what we desire — "share knowledge without creating powers".
