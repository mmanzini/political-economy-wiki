# Palantir's European backlash and the sovereignty challengers

**Source:** [Bloomberg feature, 27 July 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-07-27/palantir-rival-chapsvision-wins-french-spy-agency-contract-as-trump-ties-fray) + [Bloomberg Tech In Depth newsletter, 29 July 2026](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/newsletters/2026-07-29/palantir-s-new-rivals-in-europe-seize-the-sovereignty-opening)
**Author:** Mark Bergen, Benoit Berthelot, Zosia Wanat (feature); Mark Bergen (newsletter)

---

## Summary

Europe's digital-sovereignty movement has found its lightning rod: Palantir, "perhaps the most unabashedly Trump-aligned tech business". French PM Sébastien Lecornu announced (on Instagram) that France's domestic spy agency DGSI will replace Palantir with local challenger ChapsVision, days after Trump restricted foreign access to Anthropic's leading AI models (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's Infamy Opens Doors for French Challenger.md). A companion newsletter frames the broader trend: a wave of European startups — many founded by Palantir alumni — is pledging to do what Palantir does from Europe (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's New Rivals Seize the Sovereignty Opening in Europe.md).

## The French break

- Lecornu announced the DGSI divorce during the June week when G7 leaders were in France, declaring "France must have its own tools"; the trigger was Trump's decision four days earlier to restrict foreign access to Anthropic's frontier models (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's Infamy Opens Doors for French Challenger.md).
- Palantir was blindsided — the DGSI had signed a three-year extension in December, and a Palantir executive accused Lecornu of treating security decisions like a "Hollywood spat". Palantir France deputy CEO Pierre Lucotte said the company had been told the next renewal would go French, but "they let everyone believe that the contract was ended on the spot" (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's Infamy Opens Doors for French Challenger.md).
- France is Palantir's third-largest market after the US and UK; Palantir entered after the 2015 Bataclan attack and also serves Airbus and Stellantis (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's Infamy Opens Doors for French Challenger.md).

## The contagion across Europe

- UK MP Chi Onwurah proposed ending Palantir's £330M NHS contract, calling the company "an unacceptable point of weakness in our digital infrastructure"; some UK officials want its £240M defence deal re-evaluated (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's Infamy Opens Doors for French Challenger.md).
- Germany's domestic intelligence agency BfV picked ChapsVision to replace its Palantir contract (per Politico, May); Poland's defence ministry is evaluating European rivals; the Dutch defence minister pledged to swap Palantir for European providers where possible (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's Infamy Opens Doors for French Challenger.md).
- Bloomberg Intelligence estimates Europe must spend ~$3 trillion over the next decade on cloud, AI data centres and model training to wean itself off US and Asian suppliers; a "Buy European" procurement framework may be the EU's strongest lever. The sovereignty concern has evolved from data-storage location to fear that critical systems could be cut off in a geopolitical rupture with the US (CLOUD Act, export controls) (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's Infamy Opens Doors for French Challenger.md).

## ChapsVision — the unlikely champion

- Founded 2019 by Olivier Dellenbach (who sold eFront to BlackRock for $1.3B); named after his children's initials; pledged to keep European ownership through an eventual IPO. Italian-born ex-IBM executive Silvano Sansoni became CEO in November (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's Infamy Opens Doors for French Challenger.md).
- It is a private-equity-style roll-up: 29 acquisitions to date, combined into the Argonos data-analysis platform, now running on French cloud provider Scaleway. Growth strategy is acquisition-led market entry — "German in Germany, Italian in Italy" (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's Infamy Opens Doors for French Challenger.md).
- Scale gap: ~€200M sales last year (targeting +50% this year, €1B by 2030) and ~€350M raised, versus Palantir's $4.5B revenue, $1.6B net income and $296B market cap (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's Infamy Opens Doors for French Challenger.md).
- Analysts flag integration risk ("feature overlap and a fragmented user experience") and note full sovereignty is illusory — ChapsVision partners with Alcatel Lucent Enterprise, majority-owned by China's state-run China Huaxin (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's Infamy Opens Doors for French Challenger.md).

## The startup wave and the capability question

- Palantir alumni are seeding the challenger ecosystem: Arrakis and Valarian (both British, both with ex-Palantir co-founders) raised money in July; other alums launched healthcare and defence startups; Mistral AI has shifted its business to look more like Palantir than OpenAI (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's New Rivals Seize the Sovereignty Opening in Europe.md).
- Arrakis co-founder Rafael Quintanilla praises Palantir but calls its technology "starting to become legacy"; Comand AI's Loïc Mougeolle (battlefield software, deal with Saab) argues the real issue is "building your industrial base", while conceding European military software is not yet world-class (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's New Rivals Seize the Sovereignty Opening in Europe.md).
- Sceptics warn of strategic self-harm: retired UK general Richard Barrons — "you're going to deny yourself world-leading capabilities"; Polish analysts note Palantir's command-and-control software is proven in Ukraine war conditions and "Poland cannot afford to give up tools proven in war" (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's Infamy Opens Doors for French Challenger.md).
- The newsletter's sober verdict: Europe's tech-sovereignty movement "can't be called a success" — no evidence the continent can match Silicon Valley or China on chips, cloud or models — but Palantir-style data services are far cheaper to replicate than fabs or LLMs, making them the movement's most achievable target (source: 2026-07-29-Palantir's New Rivals Seize the Sovereignty Opening in Europe.md).

## Key Takeaways

- Trump's weaponisation of US tech access (the Anthropic export restriction, reported US stake in OpenAI) converted Europe's abstract sovereignty debate into procurement decisions; Palantir, politically the most exposed US firm, is the first casualty.
- The replacement layer is data-analytics services, not chips or models — the one tier of the stack where €350M challengers can plausibly compete with a $296B incumbent.
- The capability trade-off is real and unresolved: war-proven software versus sovereign control, with national-security veterans warning against "the temptation to isolationism".
- Watch the pattern: alumni diaspora (Arrakis, Valarian, Comand AI) borrowing Palantir's forward-deployed playbook while flying European flags.
