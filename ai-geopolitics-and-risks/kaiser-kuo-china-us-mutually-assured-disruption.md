# Kaiser Kuo on China-US 'mutually assured disruption'

**Source:** [Altri Orienti live at The Last Question, Rome (Aug 2026)](https://podcasttranscript.ai/library/cina-e-stati-uniti-un-confronto-totale-altri)
**Speaker:** Kaiser Kuo — ex-director of international communications at Baidu; host of the Sinica Podcast (since 2010); currently Beijing-based.

---

## Summary

Kaiser Kuo interviewed at the "Altri Orienti" live summit in Rome. Covers the current China-US standoff, China's emergence as a genuine tech ecosystem, Star Trek vs Black Mirror cultural framing of AI optimism, a four-quadrant scenario matrix for the AI race outcome, and why cognitive empathy is the missing ingredient in China-EU relations.

## China-US: 'mutually assured disruption'

The current state is not a cold war and not normalisation — Kaiser calls it "mutually assured disruption" (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti). Each side holds the other in a chokehold: the US can deny critical technology inputs to China; China can restrict rare-earth exports and critical minerals that the US cannot tolerate losing. The 2024-25 crisis demonstrated that China can absorb disruptions the US cannot, which produced a temporary truce — but with Donald Trump as a variable, stability is never assumed (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti).

**China after Trump 1.0.** Beijing learned from the first Trump administration. Rather than waiting to be forced to cave, China proactively reduced its technological choke points, pursued more autarky, and developed its own capabilities across strategic domains. In Kaiser's assessment, China is in a "much better position" now than it was in 2017-20 — economically and strategically (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti).

**US elite still clear-eyed.** Despite some rhetoric about American decline, Chinese strategic elites do not genuinely believe it. They understand the US still has: a talent pool drawn from the entire world (not just 1.4bn); the best research universities; considerable soft power; important alliance systems. The "paper tiger" talk is rhetorical, not policy (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti).

## American admiration for China's competence

Since January 2025 a "vibe shift" among young Americans (18-30) has made China's state capacity and infrastructure-delivery admired rather than dismissed. This spans the full political spectrum (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti):

- **Left (abundance movement):** admires manufacturing capability, ability to deliver services and infrastructure.
- **Libertarian centre:** admires technocratic competence and state coordination.
- **Populist right:** perceives cultural homogeneity as a model.

Trump himself has silenced the China-hawk faction in the administration — recognising that China-bashing is no longer a winning political strategy when China can retaliate (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti).

## China as full tech ecosystem: the innovation myth

The Western assumption that innovation requires liberal democracy is historically weak. Kaiser's examples: the Soviet Union launched Sputnik and Yuri Gagarin; the V2 rocket was developed under Nazi Germany; Leonardo worked for authoritarian courts. You don't need to know what happened in Tiananmen Square to build a good smartphone app (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti).

China's path from 2000 (below sub-Saharan-Africa per-capita GDP) to technological superpower reflects the maturation of a complete ecosystem: all layers in place, state acting as an effective coordinator, incentivising the right kinds of innovation. The specific advantage the state provides: energy. China's electrical grid is continental-scale, ultra-high voltage, increasingly renewable — solving the one bottleneck that "bedevils AI in the United States" (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti).

**DeepSeek** is not a one-off. Kaiser lists it alongside Z.ai, Jirupo (large HK IPO), and the many Chinese-surnamed researchers at leading AI conferences in Boston, Silicon Valley, Austin, and China itself. The leading AI surnames at major conferences are Zhang, Yang, Wang, Chen, Li (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti).

## Star Trek vs Black Mirror

In China, technology is associated with improving material conditions. Every person who has lived through the last 40 years has seen their life improve in lockstep with the device in their pocket. The correlation — causal or not — produces baseline trust rather than suspicion (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti).

Deeper historical root: China's "century of humiliation" is understood as a technology story. Falling behind technologically meant losing sovereignty, having cities pillaged, land ceded. The culture therefore encodes technological progress as existential necessity — the opposite of the Western Black Mirror unease about where it leads (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti).

Social confirmation: in China, academic excellence in STEM translates into social capital, economic power, and political standing. Nerd empowerment rather than nerd marginalisation (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti).

## 2x2 AI race scenario matrix

Kaiser's scenario-planning framework — two axes, both high-impact / high-uncertainty:

- **Axis 1: Who wins the AI race** (China's diffusion strategy vs US headlong rush to AGI)?
- **Axis 2: What kind of advantage does AI confer** — compounding and winner-take-all, or diffuse like electricity/the internet?

Four quadrants (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti):

| | AI = compounding (winner locks in) | AI = diffuse (fast followers catch up) |
|---|---|---|
| **China wins** | Nightmare scenario for Anthropic/Pentagon: China's governance standards, models, and approach become global. | Most likely trajectory: China wins the Global South via diffusion, drives down token/training costs, energy advantage proves decisive. |
| **US wins** | Anthropic/Sam Altman dream; very speculative. | US reaches AGI first, but open-source/open-weight models (partially driven by China's cost-reduction) diffuse globally; no party has an overweaning advantage; transformative at micro level. |

Kaiser's read: the US-wins-compounding scenario is "very science fiction"; the most probable path is China winning the diffusion race to the Global South while the US retains frontier-model leadership.

## Cognitive empathy: China-EU

For China-EU relations, Kaiser's prescription is "cognitive empathy" — understanding how your words land on the other side's ear, rooted in their lived experience and historical memory (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti).

- **Brussels fails** at this: describing China's overcapacity as "cheating", "dumping", "unfair business practices" activates the Chinese emotional register *wei qu* — the feeling of being wrongly accused when you believe you're doing the right thing. China believes it's been playing by the rule book the West wrote: saving, investing in infrastructure, producing quality goods at low prices, expanding EV and solar output.
- **Friedrich Merz's "plaza accord" reference** was particularly damaging: in Chinese public memory the 1985 Plaza Accord means "the West ganged up on an Asian country, forced currency revaluation, and sent its economy into the lost decades." Merz likely did not intend that read; Chinese policy-makers heard it that way.
- **Beijing also fails** at cognitive empathy: not understanding that out-of-work auto workers in Saxony are a political fuel for the AfD, that this is exactly what Brussels fears, that this has a direct domestic political consequence in Germany (source: kaiser-sinica-altri-orienti).

## Key Takeaways

- Current China-US state is "mutually assured disruption" — temporary truce, not stability; China can absorb disruptions the US cannot.
- China has learned from Trump 1.0: fewer choke points, more technological autonomy, stronger position than 2017.
- American admiration for China's state capacity is now bipartisan and spanning generations.
- Innovation does not require liberal democracy — China built a full tech ecosystem with state coordination and world-beating energy infrastructure.
- Star Trek vs Black Mirror: Chinese AI optimism is historically rooted, not ideological — technology = survival narrative from the century of humiliation.
- 2x2 matrix: most likely path is China winning the Global South on diffusion, not a decisive US AGI victory.
- Cognitive empathy is missing on both sides in China-EU trade tensions — key EU vocabulary activates Chinese *wei qu*; China misunderstands European domestic political constraints.
