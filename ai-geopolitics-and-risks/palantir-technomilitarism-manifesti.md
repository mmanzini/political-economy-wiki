# Palantir and the Technomilitarist Manifesto — Ep.131 "Manifesti"

**Source:** [Ep.131 — Manifesti (Fuori da Qui)](https://podcasttranscript.ai/library/ep-131-manifesti)
**Author:** Simone Pieranni (host), Piero Colaprico (featured)
**Published:** 2026-05-09

---

## Summary

The podcast "Fuori da Qui" traces Palantir's transformation from a post-9/11 civil-liberties-guardian narrative to an openly technomilitarist actor. Three inflection points drove the internal rupture: Palantir's ICE contracts enabling deportations (including the killing of a nursing protester in Minneapolis), its military targeting software's role in a US strike on an Iranian school killing 120 children, and CEO Alex Carp's 22-point manifesto explicitly rejecting pacifist ethics and labelling some cultures "dysfunctional and harmful." A concurrent Chatham House report (April 2026) confirms this mutation is systemic: the world is fragmenting into a multipolar, securitised AI landscape, and every country is now building sovereign AI infrastructure to avoid US dependency.

## Palantir's Evolution

Palantir was co-founded by Peter Thiel after 9/11 with CIA seed funding; its name comes from Tolkien's all-seeing crystal spheres that ultimately corrupt their users (source: 2026-05-09-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Manifesti.md). Its internal narrative for two decades: engineers working from within to prevent surveillance from violating civil liberties (source: 2026-05-09-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Manifesti.md).

The rupture happened on two fronts (source: 2026-05-09-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Manifesti.md):
- **Domestic**: Palantir became the technological backbone of ICE's deportation machine. During protests against ICE operations in Minneapolis, agents shot and killed a nurse. Internal Slack debate was silenced via auto-deletion of messages after 7 days.
- **International**: On the first day of the US war on Iran, a missile strike on an Iranian elementary school killed 120 children. Investigations cited Palantir's Maven targeting system as part of the strike infrastructure (source: needs-verification — this is a podcast claim; investigations cited as Wired reporting).

When employees demanded transparency on Maven, management offered NDAs in exchange for information. CEO Alex Carp, in a pre-recorded internal interview, refused to address ICE directly and suggested employees sign NDAs before receiving details (source: 2026-05-09-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Manifesti.md).

## The 22-Point Manifesto

In April 2026, Palantir's official X account published a 22-point summary of Carp's 2025 book "La Repubblica Tecnologica" (co-written with Nicola Szzamisca). *The Verge* described it as "a disturbing hybrid of deeply reactionary ideology and toxic Reddit-era forum comments, with the added weight of being the programmatic manifesto of a company that manages government data and military targeting systems" (source: 2026-05-09-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Manifesti.md).

Three pillars (source: 2026-05-09-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Manifesti.md):

1. **The end of software, the exaltation of digital weapons**: "We must rebel against the tyranny of apps." The iPhone limited our sense of the possible. Democratic societies need Hard Power built on software. "The question is not whether AI-based weapons will be built, but who will build them and for what purpose."
2. **Mandatory military service**: Democratic armies of volunteers are insufficient for the next war; governments should seriously consider reintroducing conscription.
3. **Cultural hierarchy as justification for deportation**: "Not all cultures are equal. Some have produced vital progress; others remain dysfunctional and regressive." By declaring some cultures "dysfunctional and harmful," the manifesto provides ideological cover for the ICE targeting it engineers (source: 2026-05-09-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Manifesti.md).

The manifesto represents the completion of Silicon Valley's mutation from "pacifist utopia" to "techno-military complex" (source: 2026-05-09-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Manifesti.md).

## Chatham House 2026 Report: Four Macro Trends

Research by Catia Bego (Chatham House, April 2026) confirms Palantir is not an anomaly but the vanguard (source: 2026-05-09-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Manifesti.md):

1. **Patriotic Technology**: Commercial AI companies are now organically incorporated into national security ecosystems. The US DoD launched Gen AI Mill in December 2025 — generative AI access for 3M employees integrating private-company models. China has had military-civilian fusion as official doctrine.

2. **Dual-Use Boom**: Global military spending is at Cold War-era peaks. Ukraine functioned as a massive AI war laboratory — low-cost drone swarms and satellite systems demonstrated that future wars are won via commercial technology ecosystems.

3. **Sovereign AI**: Dependency on US tech infrastructure is now perceived as an existential national security threat. Trigger events (source: 2026-05-09-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Manifesti.md):
   - Feb 2025: Trump threatened to cut Ukraine's Starlink access to force mineral concession negotiations
   - Post-ICC sanctions: American services were deactivated for the International Criminal Court's lead prosecutor
   - Cloud Act: US law allows US authorities to compel tech companies to hand over user data stored abroad
   
   Responses: Netherlands DoD building its own classified cloud; France abandoning Microsoft Teams/Zoom for sovereign platforms; Austrian military switching to LibreOffice; ASML investing €1B+ in French Mistral AI; Greenland and Taiwan sourcing satellite coverage from European consortia (source: 2026-05-09-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Manifesti.md).

4. **The Financial Bubble Risk**: In 2025, Alphabet/Amazon/Microsoft/Meta spent $300B on data center and chip infrastructure. 2026 projections: $700B. Deutsche Bank warns these companies need to recover an $800B hole by 2028 (source: 2026-05-09-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Manifesti.md). If the AI commercial bubble bursts, attention shifts from expensive frontier models to cheap open-source alternatives — advantaging China and smaller actors already betting on open-source.

## The Strategic Logic of Technomilitarism

The podcast's closing argument: Carp's manifesto is not just ideological — it is commercial survival strategy (source: 2026-05-09-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Manifesti.md). If the commercial AI market fails to repay the infrastructure investments, the only path to survival is becoming "too geopolitically important to fail" — making governments subsidise losses. The strategy: become indispensable enough to national security that the state bails you out. Palantir and similar companies are therefore structurally incentivised to promote permanent conflict and make civilian ethics appear as weakness.

## Key Takeaways

- Palantir's technomilitarist shift is not aberrant — the Chatham House report frames it as the new structural normal across major tech companies
- The "patriotic technology" trend fuses civil and military AI at the organisational level: companies, not just governments, are becoming defence actors
- Sovereign AI is now a rational defensive response: tech infrastructure dependency is a geopolitical weapon
- The potential $800B debt burden on frontier-AI companies creates systemic incentives to court military contracts — the commercial and military trajectories are converging, not by ideology alone but by financial necessity
- The open-source model trajectory (DeepSeek, cheap fine-tuning) would, if successful, undercut the very concentrated infrastructure play these companies depend on — the Chatham House bubble scenario is also a disruption scenario for technomilitary concentration
