# Japan's 2026 Intelligence Reform — From Spy Paradise to National Intelligence Bureau

**Source:** [Al Tre Orienti, Ep. 173 — Spie giapponesi](https://podcasttranscript.ai/library/ep-173-spie-giapponesi)
**Author:** Simone Piranni (Cora Media)

---

## Summary

Japan's post-WWII deliberate intelligence vacuum — born from fear of replicating the pre-war Tokkō police state — left the country chronically dependent on the US and vulnerably nicknamed a "spy paradise." On 27 May 2026, PM Sanai Takaiichi passed Japan's most radical intelligence reform since 1945: a National Intelligence Bureau replacing the toothless Cabinet Research Office, and a National Intelligence Council chaired by the PM, centralising fragmented agencies and ending US intelligence dependency. The reform has drawn China's alarm — Japan's rearmament trajectory, combined with Takaiichi's framing of Taiwan as a Japanese national security issue, is a direct geopolitical complication for Beijing's Taiwan calculus.

## The DFS / Dempabu: Japan's Secret NSA Equivalent

- The **DFS (Dempabu, literally "electromagnetic waves division")** is Japan's signal intelligence agency, headquartered at Ichigaya C1, Tokyo (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- 1,700 employees; 11 departments operating in near-total mutual isolation — separate locks and access codes per department, communications between departments almost non-existent (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- So classified that most politicians — including government ministers — are unaware of its activities; no meaningful parliamentary oversight; not subject to independent audit (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- Operational HQ: **Tachiarai Air Base, Kyushu** — giant parabolic dish antennae concealed under white "golf ball" domes; linked to the NSA for SIGINT collection since the Cold War (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- A 2008 NSA memorandum (Snowden archive) described Japanese intelligence partners as still "operating in a Cold War mindset and quite closed off" (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)

## Operation MALLARD: Mass Surveillance Without Intelligence Utility

- A 2013 NSA PowerPoint (leaked via Snowden) revealed **Operation MALLARD**: DFS analysts intercepted and archived 200,000 internet sessions per week at baseline (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- During a December 2012–January 2013 cyber-attack alert, the collection rate was raised to **500,000 sessions per hour** — 12 million sessions per day — without targeted selection (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- Despite this mass intake, analysts identified **only one email** linked to an actual cyberattack — a defining illustration of the gap between surveillance volume and intelligence utility (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- To manage the data deluge, Japan obtained **XKeyscore** from the NSA — the global real-time search engine covering emails, browsing history, and chat content — despite its incompatibility with Japanese privacy law (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- Snowden (NSA contractor in Japan 2009–2012) noted DFS appeared to target entire ISPs rather than individual suspects (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)

## Post-WWII Intelligence Void: The Tokkō Stigma

- The **Tokkō** (Special Higher Police, est. 1910) was repurposed in the 1930s into a lethal political secret police — mass imprisonment, systematic torture, suppression of all civil rights; historically compared to the Gestapo (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- After 1945, the Supreme Allied Command dissolved the Tokkō and military intelligence branches. Japan deliberately chose to outsource security to the US via the Mutual Defense Treaty — no autonomous intelligence — as a constitutional guarantee against the police state's return (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- Every attempt to pass anti-espionage legislation met mass protest: the 1985 Nakasone bill (death penalty for leaking state secrets) was withdrawn under Socialist Party, union, and bar association opposition; even Abe's 2013 Specially Designated Secrets Act triggered a 10-point polling crash (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)

## Levchenko's Testimony: Japan as "Spy Paradise" (1982)

- **Stanislav Levchenko**, a KGB officer posted to Tokyo under journalist cover until his 1979 defection, testified to the US Congress in 1982 that he had personally managed a network of over 200 high-level Japanese collaborators — including senior journalists, a former Socialist Party president, and a former Labour minister (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- Tokyo prosecutors knew of many contacts but were legally paralysed — no espionage law, no state-secrets framework, no tools to arrest or prosecute (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- Levchenko coined the phrase **"spy paradise"** — a label Washington used as leverage against Tokyo for decades, and that made Japan a perceived weak link in the Five Eyes–adjacent alliance (US, UK, Canada, Australia, New Zealand) (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)

## The May 2026 Reform: Centralisation and Strategic Autonomy

On **27 May 2026**, Japan's upper house (Diet) passed the most comprehensive intelligence reform since 1945 (the lower house had approved it in April):

- **Abolished**: the Cabinet Research Office (est. 1986) — nominal coordinator that lacked legal authority to compel ministries to share data (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- **Created**: **National Intelligence Bureau** — true central intelligence agency with binding coordination authority over the Foreign Ministry, Defence Ministry, National Police Agency, and Public Security Intelligence Agency (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- **Created**: **National Intelligence Council** — chaired by PM Takaiichi, composed of key cabinet ministers; provides strategic direction and accountability (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- The DFS is retained but integrated into a centralised, fully Japanese-controlled structure rather than operating as a US-dependent silo (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- **PM Takaiichi** (elected February 2026 with a strong mandate) framed the reform as essential in a "completely changed geopolitical context": China's Taiwan assertiveness, North Korean missile provocations, and global disorder (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)

## Civil Liberties Concerns

- The 2013 Specially Designated Secrets Act already granted sweeping classification powers over defence- and security-related data (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- The 2026 reform extends classification to **economic national security information** — a significant broadening (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- **Article 25** of the state secrets law criminalises not only the leaking official, but anyone who "solicited, induced, or coerced" the leak — threatening investigative journalists, academics, and activists with criminal prosecution (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- Human Rights Watch and the Japan Civil Liberties Union raised alarms; historical precedent: in the 1970s, the Tokyo Supreme Court convicted a Mainichi Shimbun reporter for revealing Okinawa return negotiations (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)

## China's Response and the Taiwan Dimension

- Japan is on a trajectory to become the **world's third-largest military spender** after the US and China — the intelligence reform is one pillar of a broader rearmament programme (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- Takaiichi's earlier public framing of Taiwan as a **Japanese national security issue** triggered a diplomatic incident with China; Beijing reads the intelligence reform in the same context (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- China's Global Times amplified domestic Japanese opposition, framing the reform as part of Japan's accelerating rearmament (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)
- For Beijing, the triple signal — rising military spending + intelligence centralisation + PM's Taiwan language — complicates China's Taiwan calculus by introducing Japan as a credible security actor in the island's orbit (source: 2026-06-06-AI Podcast Transcript & Summary - Spie giapponesi.md)

## Key Takeaways

- Japan's deliberate intelligence void was a rational post-WWII choice (Tokkō stigma) that became a chronic strategic liability — mass surveillance without effective analysis, and dependency on the NSA for even basic intelligence processing
- The 2026 reform is the culmination of a 40-year legislative struggle: Nakasone (1985) failed, Abe (2013) barely survived a 10-point polling crash, Takaiichi (2026) moved quickly on a strong mandate and a changed geopolitical reality
- The civil liberties risk is historically grounded: Japan's Article 25 threatens the same actors — journalists, academics, activists — that the old imperial state targeted
- China's concern is less about intelligence reform per se and more about cumulative trajectory: military spending + Taiwan framing + autonomous intelligence = Japan becoming a security actor in the Taiwan theatre
